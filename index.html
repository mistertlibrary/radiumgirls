Designing for the Lobby

Your audience reads on the move. Design for a glance, then reward a closer look. These pointers come from the Smithsonian's Guidelines for Accessible Exhibition Design (opens in a new tab).

Write It Short

  • Keep each label to 75 to 100 words.
  • Aim for sentences of about 15 words; never use more than 25.
  • One idea per sentence. Use the active voice.
  • Open each panel with a short overview in large print.

Make It Readable

  • Use a sans serif or simple serif typeface. Skip condensed, light, and script faces.
  • Never set text in all caps.
  • Print on solid backgrounds, never over a photo.
  • Choose matte paper to cut glare.

Hang It Right

  • Center wall labels about 54 inches from the floor so seated and standing visitors can both read them.
  • Let readers get close. Some visitors with low vision read from 3 inches away.

Show Them with Care

These women were real. Before you use a photograph of illness, ask: Does it serve the story or only shock? How would she want to be remembered? Who in your audience might be looking at a relative?

Use Images You May Use

Works published in the United States in 1930 or earlier are now in the public domain (opens in a new tab). That includes most newspaper pages from the 1920s. Read the rights statement on every image anyway.

Credit Everything

Every photograph and quotation on your board needs a short credit line. Keep a full works cited list in MLA on the back of your trifold. Citation Station (opens in a new tab) can help.
